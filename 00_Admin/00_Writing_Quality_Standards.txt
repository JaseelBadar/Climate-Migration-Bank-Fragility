TITLE FORMULA
================================================================================

BAD TITLES (too vague):
❌ "Climate Change and Banking Stability"
❌ "An Analysis of Deposit Flows in India"
❌ "Night Lights and Financial Outcomes"

GOOD TITLES (specific + mechanism):
✅ "Shadow Runs in the Monsoon: Climate Shocks, Migration, and Banking 
    Fragility in India"
✅ "Night Lights as a Migration Proxy: Evidence from Flood-Induced Deposit 
    Withdrawals"
✅ "Climate Shocks and Liquidity Stress: Satellite Evidence from Indian 
    District Banks"

TITLE TEST:
- Does it tell me the WHAT (climate shocks), WHERE (India), and HOW (night lights)?
- Does it hint at the mechanism (migration, shadow runs)?
- Is it searchable? (Keywords: climate, banking, India, migration)

ABSTRACT FORMULA (150 words, 7 sentences)
================================================================================

SENTENCE 1: Big question
"Do climate shocks trigger liquidity stress in banking systems through 
 migration channels?"

SENTENCE 2: Why this is hard
"Migration is unobserved, and identifying causal effects on bank deposits 
 requires addressing reverse causality and confounders."

SENTENCE 3: Your innovation
"I use VIIRS nighttime lights as a proxy for migration-driven economic activity 
 and instrument with exogenous flood exposure from EM-DAT disaster data."

SENTENCE 4: Sample description
"Using a panel of 666 Indian districts from 2015-2024 (23,347 district-quarters), 
 I find..."

SENTENCE 5: Main result (with magnitude)
"...that a 10% decline in nighttime lights predicts a 28% deposit withdrawal, 
 with timing consistent with liquidity stress rather than credit deterioration."

SENTENCE 6: Mechanism/heterogeneity
"Effects are concentrated in urban districts and areas with low deposit insurance 
 coverage, suggesting shadow-run dynamics."

SENTENCE 7: Contribution/policy implication
"Results quantify a novel climate-banking transmission channel and provide an 
 early-warning framework using satellite data for macroprudential regulation."

QUALITY CHECK:
□ No jargon in first 3 sentences (accessible to non-specialists)
□ Numbers are specific (not "significant effect" but "28% withdrawal")
□ Contribution is clear (what's new? why does it matter?)

INTRODUCTION STRUCTURE (4-5 pages)
================================================================================

PARAGRAPH 1: Motivation with vivid example
"In August 2018, Kerala experienced its worst floods in a century. Over 400 
 people died, 1.4 million were displaced, and economic losses exceeded $4 billion. 
 Less visibly, district banks reported sharp deposit withdrawals in flood-affected 
 areas—not from bank failures, but from depositors relocating or withdrawing cash 
 for immediate needs. This paper investigates whether such climate-induced 
 migration triggers systematic liquidity stress in the banking system."

PARAGRAPH 2: Literature gap (3-part gap)
"Existing research documents climate impacts on agriculture (cite), migration 
 flows (cite), and bank credit (cite), but three gaps remain. First, no study 
 links climate shocks to bank DEPOSITS through migration channels. Second, 
 migration data limitations have prevented causal identification. Third, the 
 TIMING of deposit responses—immediate (liquidity) vs. delayed (credit 
 deterioration)—remains unexamined."

PARAGRAPH 3: Research question + preview of answer
"I ask: Do climate shocks cause deposit withdrawals through migration channels? 
 Using Indian district-level data from 2015-2024, I find that floods reduce 
 nighttime lights by 1.25% and deposits by 28% (instrumented), with effects 
 occurring within one quarter—a timing pattern consistent with shadow runs, 
 not slow-moving credit losses."

PARAGRAPH 4: Contribution 1 - Methodological
"First contribution: I introduce nighttime lights as a migration proxy for 
 financial outcomes. Unlike census data (infrequent) or mobile phone data 
 (proprietary), VIIRS satellite imagery provides monthly, publicly available 
 coverage at 463m resolution, enabling district-quarter panel analysis."

PARAGRAPH 5: Contribution 2 - Substantive mechanism
"Second contribution: I document a shadow-run mechanism—rapid deposit withdrawals 
 triggered by perceived liquidity stress, not bank insolvency. This differs from 
 classic bank runs (Diamond-Dybvig 1983) where fundamental deterioration causes 
 panic. Here, migration ITSELF causes withdrawals, even when banks remain solvent."

PARAGRAPH 6: Contribution 3 - Policy relevance
"Third contribution: I quantify the climate-banking transmission channel for 
 macroprudential policy. The RBI currently monitors credit risk from climate 
 disasters but not liquidity risk from migration. My results suggest satellite 
 data can provide early warnings of deposit stress, enabling pre-emptive liquidity 
 injections."

PARAGRAPH 7: Roadmap
"Section 2 reviews literature. Section 3 describes data. Section 4 outlines 
 identification strategy. Section 5 presents results. Section 6 discusses 
 mechanisms and policy implications. Section 7 concludes."

WRITING RULES FOR INTRODUCTION:
- No equations (save for Methods section)
- No abbreviations without definition (first mention: "VIIRS (Visible Infrared 
  Imaging Radiometer Suite)")
- Every claim must have citation or forward reference to your result
- End each paragraph with transition to next ("However...", "To address this...", 
  "Building on this...")

METHODS SECTION (Identification) - MOST IMPORTANT FOR REVIEWERS
================================================================================

STRUCTURE:

Subsection 4.1: OLS is Biased (Why We Need IV)
"Regressing deposits on nighttime lights yields biased estimates due to reverse 
 causality (economic growth increases both lights and deposits) and omitted 
 variables (urbanization affects both). To address this, I exploit exogenous 
 variation from flood shocks."

Subsection 4.2: IV Strategy
"I instrument ΔLights with flood exposure. The identifying assumption is that 
 floods affect deposits ONLY through their effect on lights (migration/activity), 
 not through direct banking disruption. I test this assumption by [checking if 
 bank branches remain operational during floods using RBI branch-level data]."

Subsection 4.3: Threats to Validity + How I Address Them

THREAT 1: Direct disruption (floods damage bank buildings)
RESPONSE: "I verify that 94% of bank branches in flood-affected districts 
           remained operational during disaster months (RBI data), suggesting 
           direct disruption is minimal."

THREAT 2: Selection (floods hit poorer districts with weaker banks)
RESPONSE: "I control for district fixed effects, absorbing time-invariant 
           differences. Pre-trends test shows no differential deposit growth 
           before floods (Figure 3)."

THREAT 3: Spillovers (people move TO unaffected districts, increasing deposits there)
RESPONSE: "I test for spatial spillovers using adjacent-district exposure. 
           Coefficients are insignificant (Table 7), suggesting localized effects."

Subsection 4.4: Why Remaining Threats Don't Invalidate Results
"Even if some direct disruption exists, it would ATTENUATE my estimates (deposits 
 fall for reasons beyond migration), making my 28% effect a LOWER BOUND. The true 
 migration effect may be larger."

REVIEWERS WILL TRY TO KILL YOUR PAPER HERE.
Pre-empt EVERY objection. Show you thought of it first.

================================================================================
RESULTS SECTION - TABLES TELL STORIES
================================================================================

EVERY TABLE NEEDS:

1. TITLE that describes the result
   ❌ "Table 3: Regression Results"
   ✅ "Table 3: Nighttime Lights Decline Predicts Deposit Withdrawals (IV Estimates)"

2. NOTES section explaining:
   - Sample (N = 23,347 district-quarters, 631 districts, 2015Q1-2024Q4)
   - Standard errors (clustered at district level)
   - Fixed effects included (district + quarter FE in all columns)
   - Stars (✱✱✱ p<0.01, ✱✱ p<0.05, ✱ p<0.10)
   - Interpretation (Column 2: 10% lights decline → 28% deposit withdrawal)

3. LOGICAL COLUMN PROGRESSION
   Column 1: OLS (biased, for comparison)
   Column 2: IV (main result)
   Column 3: First stage (show instrument is strong, F-stat)
   Column 4: Reduced form (floods → deposits directly)
   Column 5: Robustness (alternate sample or controls)

IN-TEXT TABLE DISCUSSION:
❌ "Table 3 shows the results."
✅ "Table 3 presents IV estimates. Column 2 shows that a 10% decline in nighttime 
    lights predicts a 28% deposit withdrawal (p=0.02). The first stage (Column 3) 
    is strong (F-stat=968), satisfying the relevance condition. The reduced form 
    (Column 4) confirms floods directly reduce deposits, consistent with the IV 
    mechanism."

DISCUSSION SECTION - ADDRESS LIMITATIONS HEAD-ON
================================================================================

SUBSECTION: "Limitations and Interpretation"

"Four limitations warrant discussion. First, 49% of flood events are coded at 
 state level (EM-DAT data constraint), creating measurement error that biases 
 estimates toward zero. My effects are thus LOWER BOUNDS. Second, VIIRS measures 
 economic activity, not population directly. However, the strong first stage 
 (F=968) suggests lights capture migration-driven changes sufficient for financial 
 outcome prediction. Third, I lack individual-level deposit data, preventing 
 analysis of within-district heterogeneity. Fourth, I cannot distinguish temporary 
 from permanent migration; both would reduce deposits, but policy responses differ."

TURN LIMITATIONS INTO FUTURE RESEARCH:
"Future work could combine VIIRS with mobile phone CDR data to distinguish 
 displacement patterns, or use bank-level microdata to test heterogeneity by 
 bank type and depositor characteristics."

REVIEWERS RESPECT HONESTY.
Hiding limitations guarantees rejection. Disclosing them invites constructive feedback.

WRITING QUALITY CHECKLIST (Apply to EVERY paragraph)
================================================================================

□ First sentence states the point (topic sentence)
□ Evidence follows immediately (cite or reference table)
□ No sentence exceeds 30 words (break into two if longer)
□ No paragraph exceeds 6 sentences (break into two if longer)
□ Transition to next paragraph is clear
□ No jargon without definition (first use: spell out acronym)
□ Active voice preferred ("I find" not "It is found that")
□ Numbers are specific ("28% decline" not "large decline")
□ Every claim is defensible (can cite evidence or theory)

READ ALOUD TEST:
If you stumble while reading aloud, readers will stumble too. Rewrite.

================================================================================
SENTENCE-LEVEL EDITING RULES
================================================================================

WORDINESS:
❌ "In light of the fact that" → ✅ "Because"
❌ "It is important to note that" → ✅ DELETE (just state the point)
❌ "The results suggest that there may be" → ✅ "Results suggest"

PRECISION:
❌ "impacts" → ✅ "affects" or "correlates with" (depending on causality)
❌ "significant" → ✅ "statistically significant" or "economically meaningful"
❌ "proves" → ✅ "suggests" (unless experimental proof)

CLARITY:
❌ "The aforementioned methodology" → ✅ "This approach"
❌ "Utilization of" → ✅ "Using"
❌ "In order to" → ✅ "To"

FINAL PRE-SUBMISSION CHECKLIST
================================================================================

□ Abstract under 150 words, no abbreviations
□ Introduction ends with clear roadmap
□ Every table/figure cited in text in order (Table 1, then 2, then 3...)
□ Methods section pre-empts all identification concerns
□ Results section interprets magnitude, not just significance
□ Discussion discloses limitations honestly
□ Conclusion states policy implications clearly
□ References formatted consistently (journal style)
□ Appendix includes all robustness checks
□ Figures are publication-ready (300dpi, clear labels, readable in grayscale)

FINAL READ:
Print the paper. Read it on paper, not screen. Mark every sentence that feels 
unclear, defensive, or inflated. Rewrite those sentences.

If you wouldn't want your name on a sentence, delete it.
